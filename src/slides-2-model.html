Model·Hardware templates

Define a model of hardware once. Every device inherits its ports.

Device types are templates

A device type lists the interfaces, console ports, power ports and bays a model has. Create a device of that type and all its components appear, named and numbered. Edit the template and new devices pick up the change.

models/dcim/devicetypedevicetype-library

Modules for modular kit

Chassis with line cards, transceivers or expansion cards use module bays and module types. Installing a module can create its interfaces with the right names.

models/dcim/moduletype

New in 4.7: bay types and moves

Module bay types declare what fits where, for example an SFP28 cage or a PCIe x16 slot, and NetBox validates that a module suits its bay. An installed module, with its whole subtree, can now be moved to another bay instead of being deleted and recreated.

New in 4.7: lifecycle date

Device types and module types gain an end_of_life date. Filter on it and you have a hardware refresh list without a separate spreadsheet.

source: v4.7 release notes · the community library holds ready-made device type definitions you can import

Model·Power and, new in 4.7, cooling

Power was always modelled end to end. Cooling now mirrors it.

Power chain

SourcePower panelSite or location
CircuitPower feedTo a rack
DevicePower portDraw and capacity

Panels, feeds, power ports and outlets let you check a rack's budget before you rack the next server.

Cooling chain, new in 4.7

SourceCooling sourceChiller, tower, dry cooler
LoopCooling feedCoolant to a rack
DeviceIntakeCold plate or CDU inlet

Coolant distribution units and manifolds are ordinary devices that carry cooling intakes and outflows.

Prefer to keep it light?

You do not have to model the plumbing. A cooling_method of air, liquid, hybrid or immersion is available on devices and device types, and racks can record cooling_capability and cooling_capacity. That is enough to plan liquid-cooled capacity without drawing every pipe.

Model·Clusters and virtual machines

Hypervisors are devices. Guests are virtual machines, on the same IP plan.

Clusters

Group hosts into a cluster of a type you define, for example VMware, Proxmox or Kubernetes, optionally scoped to a site. Hypervisors stay ordinary devices in racks.

models/virtualization/cluster

Virtual machines

Record vCPUs, memory, disks, role, platform and status. Give them interfaces and assign IPs from the same prefixes your physical kit uses.

models/virtualization/virtualmachine

One search for everything

Search an IP address and find the VM, its cluster, the host it sits on and the rack that host is in. That chain is the whole point of modelling both sides.

Be honest about scope: community NetBox stores what you tell it. It does not read vCenter or a cloud account for you. Teams populate it from their platform with scripts or the API, which the integration chapter covers.
